Essay · 2026-09-10 · lectr.bid

130 minutes to 52,
without changing a number.

lectr rebuilds its whole market every night: crawl sixteen houses, reassemble a 1.1M-lot corpus, rerun the value engine, ship. The run had crept past two hours. One evening of profiling and two rewrites brought it to 52 minutes, and the outputs are identical to what they were.

where the time went

Profile a healthy night before touching anything.

The crawl is a matrix, one job per house, all in parallel, so its wall-clock is whichever house is slowest that night. Christie's took 77 minutes on a bad one and Huggins & Scott 80. Nothing to rewrite there; that's the houses' pace, not mine.

Assemble is different. It's the only stage that sees the whole corpus, and it's serial: 71 to 95 minutes, and the profile said almost all of it was two things. A repeat-sale grouper that finds the same object sold more than once, and the per-maker hedonic price indices. Everything else combined was single-digit minutes.

repeat sales

Only compare what can physically match.

The grouper pairs lots that are the same object. It was deciding whether a lot was even eligible to match inside the pairwise loop, so the expensive check ran on every candidate pair instead of once per lot. Hoisting eligibility out, so only lots that can physically match are indexed before any pairing happens, took the stage from 1,660 seconds to 3.

It finds exactly the same pairs. That's the whole point of the next section.

indices

Solve for the columns you actually read.

Each maker gets its own hedonic regression. Two changes. The makers now run in a worker pool instead of one after another. And the covariance step was solving the full system when the printed band only ever reads about a quarter of the columns, so it now solves for those through a Cholesky factor: O(p²·q) instead of O(p³). Patek, the biggest maker, went from 572 seconds to 109. The stage went from 1,700 seconds to 87. A follow-up builds the normal-equations matrix straight from the accumulator instead of materializing the design matrix first.

the rule

A speed change ships only if the output is provably identical.

Both rewrites were run offline against the full corpus next to the old code, and the outputs were diffed before anything merged. Zero differences, for the repeat-sale pairs and for every printed index value. A faster engine that prints different numbers isn't an optimization, it's a new engine, and it would have to earn its own backtest record.

  • Repeat-sale grouping, eligibility hoisted out of the pair loop1,660s → 3s
  • Per-maker indices, worker pool + quarter-column covariance1,700s → 87s
  • Assemble stage71–95 min → 33 min
  • Full nightly, weekday110–130 min → 52 min
what's left

The crawl is now the long pole.

Christie's alone is over an hour and would need sharding to move. The valuation pass over upcoming lots is about ten minutes and is next. And the timeouts moved: crawl from 90 to 120 minutes, assemble from 120 to 180, so a slow house degrades the run instead of killing it.
