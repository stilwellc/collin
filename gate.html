Postmortem · 2026-09-09 · lectr.bid

The gate that
wedged the pipeline.

On September 2 I added a safety gate to lectr's nightly so a broken crawler could never publish poisoned prices. For the next week it blocked every publish. Prices froze, 18,797 freshly crawled lots sat unshipped, and the report that reached me was "prices aren't updating and I don't think it's ingesting." Here's what happened and what I'd tell myself.

the gate

A crawler can stamp one price on thousands of lots.

When a house's pages misbehave, a crawl can walk the wrong ids and stamp one price across a run of lots. The worst shape I'd seen was $10,050 on 3,622 NFL Auction lots. The sentinel exists to catch that: it looks for clusters of lots at one house that share an identical price, sets aside the honest ones (ties on a bid increment), and calls the rest poison.

The September 2 rule was simple. Two or more poison clusters and the publish aborts. Simple was the problem.

why it never opened

The book already looked like that.

The standing corpus carried 82 of those signatures. All old, all known, all from crawls that had long since been healed or accepted. An absolute count can't tell "the crawler broke tonight" from "the book has always had these," so the gate tripped every single night, on the same 82.

Then the second failure, which is the one anybody noticed. The intraday close-board reads the published corpus to find lots closing in the next 24 hours and reprices them. The published corpus was frozen on September 2, so it found nothing closing, so nothing moved. Two symptoms, one cause, and from the outside it looked exactly like ingestion had stopped.

the fix

Gate on the delta, not the count.

Tonight's signature set is diffed against the one from the last published corpus. Only new poison clusters count, and it still takes two of them to abort. One absolute backstop survives any baseline: a single price stamped across thousands of lots is catastrophic whether or not it was there yesterday.

The decision is a pure function now, with a test whose fixture is the real 82-signature set from the September 9 production run. The first case is literally "would last night have published under the new rule." The same night I added a dispatch input that turns the gate into a warning, so the next wedge can be opened by hand in one minute instead of a week. The engine's other gate, a holdout monotonicity check, got the same treatment: a dip is a warning that the signal degraded, not a reason to hold the site.

what I'd tell past me

A gate that isn't relative to the last good state is a countdown.

Diff against the last good publish. Any check that scores the world in absolute terms will eventually be failed by a world that is fine.

Not publishing is a failure, so make it loud. The site kept serving. It just served yesterday, then the day before, and nothing on it said so. A crash would have been found in a day.

Every gate ships with its valve. If you can't override it from the dispatch form, you don't own it, it owns you.

Test gates against production data. Synthetic fixtures pass; the real 82 didn't. The test now carries the real ones.
